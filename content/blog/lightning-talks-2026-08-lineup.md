## The lineup so far

**1. Daria Linhart Grudzien**, EuroPython Organizer and Former Site Reliability Engineer
*I Came for the Talks, I Stayed for the Volunteering*

Organizing a meetup or a conference can look like pure giving. Daria makes the case that
it gives back more than it takes, drawing on six years of organizing Python conferences
in Europe.

**2. Maksym Shalenyi**, Python Developer
*Viy*

Viy is process monitoring for Linux, built for Python. It captures CPU, memory, I/O,
network, threads, and file descriptors together, with time series visualization and
performance insights.

**3. Sophia Chu**, Software Developer, Improving Vancouver
*Agents as Sounding Boards: Psychology and AI*

We may not trust agents with write access to our repos, but we hand them our doubts and
our half-formed ideas. Sophia looks at the psychology behind that instinct and what it
does to developers and teams.

**4. Melissa Bather**, R-Ladies Vancouver Co-Organiser
*Sasquatch Statistics: Spatial Point Process Modelling in R*

Can spatial statistics explain why Bigfoot sightings cluster where they do? Melissa walks
through a spatial point regression workflow in R, using methods that apply just as well
to ecology, epidemiology, and criminology.

## Submit your own lightning talk idea

We're still accepting talk submissions for this event, so there is room for you.

Lightning talks exist because the format removes almost every reason to say no. Five to
fifteen minutes. No need for a narrative arc, a live demo, or a conclusion that ties
everything together. You explain one thing you know, and then you sit down.

Here is what we hear from people who almost submitted:

- **"I am not an expert."** Neither are most speakers. A lightning talk is not a claim of
  authority, it is a report from where you happen to be standing. The thing you learned
  last month is interesting precisely because you remember what it felt like not to know
  it.
- **"My topic is too small."** Small is the format. A function you did not know existed,
  a plotting default you always override, a package that saved you an afternoon. Five
  minutes is not enough room for a big topic anyway.
- **"Someone already gave this talk."** Not to this room, and not in your words.
- **"I already gave this talk somewhere else."** Good. Give it again. Reusing a talk is
  allowed and encouraged, and it is the least expensive way to get on a stage.
- **"I do not know what to say yet."** Submit anyway and tell us that. We will work it
  out with you.

Python or R. Either community, either language, either room.

## A joint event with R-Ladies

PyLadies Vancouver and R-Ladies Vancouver draw from overlapping communities and solve
overlapping problems, usually in separate rooms. Data work does not respect the language
boundary. Most people we know reach for both and are slightly apologetic about it.

A lightning talks night is the easiest possible way to put those rooms together. Short
talks mean nobody has to sit through forty minutes of a stack they do not use, and the
range of what counts as on topic gets much wider.
